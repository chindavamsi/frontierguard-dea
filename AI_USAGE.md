# Generative AI usage

## Disclosure

Generative AI has assisted the development of FrontierGuard DEA. OpenAI Codex
has been used for research and project planning, repository scaffolding, and
drafting or reviewing source code, tests, documentation, and release metadata.
On 2026-07-26, Codex also assisted in drafting the scholarly metadata for
release `0.1.0a1`.

The exact serving model or version was not recorded in the repository for all
earlier sessions. No model identifier is asserted here when it cannot be
verified. Future research and release records should capture the product,
model/version when exposed by the tool, date, task scope, affected artifacts,
and human verification performed.

## Human accountability

Vamsi Chinda is the creator and accountable human maintainer of FrontierGuard
DEA. Generative AI systems are tools, not authors or contributors. They do not
make release decisions, assume responsibility for the software, or supply
independent evidence that a scientific claim is correct.

Before a release or research claim is accepted, the human maintainer is
responsible for:

- deciding the scientific objectives, conventions, architecture, and release
  scope;
- reviewing and accepting or rejecting AI-assisted changes;
- checking mathematical formulations against primary literature;
- running and interpreting the project's automated quality checks;
- validating numerical results against analytical examples and independent
  implementations where available; and
- accurately reporting limitations, uncertainty, and incomplete validation.

AI-generated or AI-suggested output must be treated as untrusted until these
checks are complete. Automated tests written with AI assistance are not, by
themselves, independent validation of AI-assisted implementation code.

## Research-publication disclosure

Any manuscript based on FrontierGuard DEA should include a venue-appropriate
AI-use statement. At minimum, that statement should identify:

1. the AI product and model/version, when known;
2. the dates and purposes of use;
3. the parts of the software, analysis, or manuscript affected;
4. the human review and validation applied; and
5. the scientific and design decisions made by the human authors.

This file is a living disclosure. It should be updated when the scope or tools
used materially change.
